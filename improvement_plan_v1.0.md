## Top-Down Tactical — Improvement Plan (from v0.5)

You have built more working tactical simulation in one burndown night than most prototypes get in a month: a real ballistics model with concealment separated from cover, a corner mechanic no shipped competitor has, noise propagation, six missions on a pluggable objective framework, a compliance economy with feints. The problem is that almost none of it currently does anything, because the fight underneath it cannot be lost. A duel harness driving your real `update()` loop, 60 trials per cell, enemy pre-alerted with clean line of sight, gives the player a **100% win rate at 96, 160, 256, 384 and 512px on ROOKIE, REGULAR and ELITE — 15 of 15 cells — finishing at 100hp in every single trial.** Your TTK against a 65hp guard is 2 rounds at 640rpm, 0.094s of trigger time, against an alerted enemy reaction floor of 0.25s. The enemy's reaction window is dead time you always win. That is the one thing that most limits this game. Angles do not matter if you cannot lose the fight at the end of them; doors are not decisions if every room is free; the corner game has no stakes; the difficulty tiers have nothing to bite on; and restraint is not a trade because kills are free. Everything below is downstream of it.

Standing caveat on the whole document: **no human has played this build.** Every measurement here comes from an instrumented bot with no fire discipline, no cornering behaviour, and no use of flashbangs, charges, the mirror, the shoulder swap, or squad orders. It measures the simulation, not the game. Where a recommendation rests on an untested assumption about how a human will actually behave, I say so inline.

## Do these first

### 1. Stop the build from failing closed on a human

**What.** Three separate ways a first-time player hits a wall the bot never hits.

`closeDoor()` at line 1272 calls `addNoise(c.x, c.y, ...)` at line 1282 with an undeclared `c`. `openDoor` (1267) and `kickDoor` (1288) both declare `const c = doorCenter(d)` first; `closeDoor` does not. Under `"use strict"` this is a `ReferenceError` that unwinds `playerInteract → updatePlayer → update → frame()`, and `frame()` calls `requestAnimationFrame(frame)` as its last statement with no try/catch anywhere in the chain. The RAF loop is never rescheduled. The game freezes mid-mission with no error surface. I ran it headlessly against the live file: `closeDoor THROWS: ReferenceError: c is not defined`. And `contextPrompt()` renders `[E] close door` whenever the player stands near an open door, so the HUD instructs the player to press the key that kills the build.

The debrief is a dead end: `#debrief` contains exactly one control, `btnRestart`, wired to `startMission(true)`, which restarts the same `mapIndex` with the same loadout. `frame()` gates all keyboard handling on `game.state === "play"`, so Escape does nothing there. After finishing a mission the only path to a different mission or difficulty is to restart the one you just played, press Esc mid-fight, and scroll past the 28-binding key table. **Five of six missions are unreachable after the first completion without a page reload.**

And HIGH VALUE deploys one squaddie. Its map source places a single `s` glyph because the `XXX` extraction block occupies the tiles where the other two would go; `initGame` does `level.spawns.squad.slice(0, 3)` and silently builds a one-man squad. Keys 2 and 3 select nothing. I parsed all six maps: HIGH VALUE reports `squadSpawns=1`, every other map reports 3.

**Why.** Your next milestone is a human playing this. All three of these fire in the first thirty minutes of that session and all three read as "the game is broken", not "the game is hard."

**Cost.** Under an hour total. One line for `closeDoor`. Six lines for a Mission Select button that unhides `#menu`, sets `game.inProgress = false` and calls `refreshMenu()`. Two characters in the HIGH VALUE map source. Then a try/catch around `update(dt)` in `frame()` that logs, sets a visible SIMULATION ERROR HUD state, and still calls `requestAnimationFrame` — a single-file prototype with no error surface must never fail closed. Add the map rule to `validateMap`, which already hard-fails on row length and unknown glyphs (569-577): every map declares exactly one `P` and at least three `s`, and any map with an `extract` objective declares at least one `X`.

**How you know it worked.** You press E on an open door and the game keeps running. You finish THE COMPOUND, click through to ROOKIE, and start MARKET ROW without touching the address bar.

**The lesson worth more than the fix:** `tests/bot.js:39` only presses E or F when `d.state === 'closed'`, so no playthrough ever closed a door, and `tests/bot.js:52` wraps `update()` in its own try/catch, so even if it had, the crash would have been invisible to the harness. Add a bot case that closes doors, and remove that swallow.

### 2. Make the fight losable — put the cost on target acquisition, not on damage

**What.** Three changes to the acquisition model, none of which touch your damage numbers.

**(a) Turn bloom.** In `currentSpread()` (841), add a term proportional to recent angular velocity of `p.face`, capped near 9 degrees, decaying over ~0.25s. Whipping the mouse onto a new contact then costs 0.2-0.3s of settle, the same order as the enemy's reaction window. Pieing a corner slowly costs nothing. This is Door Kickers 2's per-trooper aim time and Ready or Not's weapon settle, and it is what makes GOAL.md's own target sentence ("I died because I rushed the door instead of pieing it") mechanically true rather than aspirational.

**(b) Exposure accumulates.** `e.reactT` is assigned only on the transition into combat (`if (e.state !== "combat")`, line 1470), and losing line of sight for one frame drops the enemy to `hunt` (1476). Awareness is a boolean with no memory, so every re-peek re-rolls the clock from zero. Measured on THE COMPOUND with the enemy pinned so hunt-drift could not contaminate the result, 12 trials of 15s per cell: **0hp taken at 0.17s, 0.25s and 0.35s in/out duty cycles, versus 470hp standing statically exposed.** Replace the boolean with `e.aware += dt` while LOS holds, `-= dt*0.4` while it does not, clamped to [0, 1.5], firing at a `reactNeed` rolled once on first sighting and never re-rolled. The sixth peek gets shot at.

**(c) Watching the door.** At the acquisition branch (1470), if the target is already within ~15 degrees of `e.face`, roll reaction from [0.10, 0.18] instead of [0.25, 0.50]. `e.face` currently gates FOV only and never touches the reaction clock, so a guard staring down the hallway you walk into is no faster than one facing a wall.

**Why.** This is the single change that gives every other system in the build something to hold. It also fixes the strafe-peek exploit before a human finds it, which they will, because it is the same input pattern every CS or Valorant player already has in their hands.

**Cost.** Medium. Maybe 60 lines across three functions, plus a tuning pass.

**How you know it worked.** Re-run the duel harness. If any cell is still 100/100 at 100hp, you have not moved the needle. Target something like: player wins ~85% at close range on REGULAR and takes damage in a third of wins, dropping toward 55-65% on ELITE at long range.

**Untested assumption:** that turn bloom reads as weight rather than as input lag. Nobody has felt it. Tune it in front of a human, not against the harness — the harness aims perfectly and instantly and will report turn bloom as a pure nerf.

### 3. Make enemies move once they are in combat

**What.** `updateEnemy`'s `case "combat"` (1482-1502) contains facing, a reaction timer, burst fire and a reload. There is no `moveAlongPath` call, no cover query, no break-contact. **Measured: an enemy moved 0.0px across 600 consecutive combat frames — ten full seconds — while dealing 501hp.** He only moves once you break LOS, when `hunt` (1503) walks him to `lastKnown`, which means walking him into your prepared angle.

Add three behaviours in value order. (1) **Break contact on damage:** in `applyHit`, when the victim is an enemy in combat, path to the nearest tile within ~120px with cover from the round's bearing (raycast `blocksBullet` from that tile toward the shooter) and suppress fire during transit. (2) **Hold the angle:** an enemy in `hunt` whose path crosses a door tile stops 4-6 tiles short on the open side and faces the door rather than walking through it. This is the one that removes the doorway camp. (3) **Pair up:** if a comrade within 250px is in combat with the same target, one holds and the other pathfinds to a tile with LOS at more than 60 degrees off the first angle.

**Why.** Right now the optimal technique on every enemy on every map is identical: expose, fire two rounds, step back behind the frame, kill him as he walks into your muzzle. Nothing in the codebase punishes it. All three behaviours sit on the existing `astar`/`moveAlongPath`.

**Cost.** Medium. The break-contact cover query is the only new geometry, and it reuses `blocksBullet`.

**How you know it worked.** Instrument average enemy displacement during combat frames. Zero is the current number; anything above ~40px per combat engagement means the funnel is contested. Then: does a bot that only ever peeks the same doorway still clear THE COMPOUND?

### 4. Make hostage rescue actually function

**What.** Two bugs, one design fix.

`parseLevel`'s flood fill (649-665) treats only walls and doors as boundaries. Window tiles have `level.wall = 0` and are not in `doorAt`, so the fill runs straight through them and merges rooms, including into the exterior. `trySecureHostage` (1640) refuses while any live un-cuffed enemy shares the flood-filled room id (1642), so the merge directly gates the rescue objective. Measured on the live build: SAFEHOUSE ROW puts both hostages in a **797-tile blob with 3 blockers**; FLIGHT 214 resolves to exactly two rooms, an 18-tile cockpit and a 1086-tile blob containing the entire cabin and the whole exterior. Add `|| level.window[ny][nx]` to the fill guard at 661 and `|| level.window[y][x]` to the seed at 652. I measured the result: SAFEHOUSE ROW's hostage room drops to 63 tiles with 1 blocker. **Bot batches, 20 runs on SAFEHOUSE ROW: stock 9/20 wins and 21 hostages secured; with the window fix 14/20 wins and 36 secured.**

That does not fix FLIGHT 214. The aisle legitimately connects every seat block, so after the window fix the cabin is still 316 tiles holding three of four hostages and all five cabin guards. Securing one hostage requires neutralising the map. **This, not the bot's fire discipline, is the structural half of "zero hostages on the airliner."** Replace the room-id test with a local one, which is what SWAT 4 actually does: refuse only if a live threat is within ~220px *with line of sight to the hostage*.

Then make securing mean something. `secured` currently sets a flag and nothing else — `updateCivilian` (1655) only ticks blind/stagger and writes `c.cower`, which nothing reads. The hostage does not move, does not go prone, is still a full-size bullet target, and `rescue.failed()` (2755) fires on `hostagesDead > 0`, so you can secure all four and lose to a stray round afterward. On secure: shrink `h.r` to 5, set `h.prone`, have `firstEntityOnSegment` skip prone hostages, walk them to the nearest entry or extraction tile over ~4s, despawn. Change `rescue.failed()` to count only unsecured hostage deaths.

**Why.** Four of six missions are rescue missions and the objective is currently gated behind clearing the entire map. And the failure is invisible: the game says "Clear the room before securing" about a room it never draws.

**Cost.** Small. Two characters for the flood fill, ~15 lines for the local gate, ~25 for the prone-and-walk-out.

**How you know it worked.** FLIGHT 214 goes above zero hostages secured in a 20-run batch. Then tint the blocking room's floor tiles using the existing per-tile `level.room` and print "ROOM NOT CLEAR — 2 hostiles" so a human can see the rule.

### 5. Give the player back the seconds where the game stops talking

**What.** `updatePlayer` gates movement on `p.stagger <= 0` (900) and firing on `p.blind <= 0 && p.stagger <= 0 && p.swapT <= 0` (914). Nothing in `drawHud()` renders either state. A flashbang thrown at a cursor inside `bangRadius` — which a first-time player will do constantly, since G throws at the cursor on a 1.0s fuse — blinds them for 1.1s and staggers them for 0.66s, during which WASD and LMB simply do nothing. A squad breach charge inside `chargeStunRadius` staggers for 2.2s. The only tell for blind is that the crosshair turns `COLORS.danger`, which is the exact colour the CHECK FIRE hazard uses, so your two most important negative states share one signal. Getting shot is equally mute: line 1163, `game.camKick += 5`, is the complete player damage response, an undirected jolt in a game where the shooter can legitimately be off-screen.

Three additions, all in `drawHud()`, which already draws in screen space:
- Full-screen white wash at `alpha = pow(p.blind / TUNE.bangBlindFriendly, 0.5)`, plus a status word beside the shoulder line ("FLASHED 1.1s" / "STUNNED"). Separate the colours: hazard stays red with the CHECK FIRE label; blind becomes a white bloomed crosshair with no gap information, because you cannot aim.
- A directional damage arc: on `applyHit` push `{ang: b.ang + PI, t: 0.9}` onto `p.hurt` and stroke a 40-degree arc ~120px from screen centre. This is the correct answer to the off-screen fairness measurement (4% of rounds that hit the player came from an off-screen shooter, p90 hit range 356px against a 250px vertical half-extent).
- On blind, start a 4.2kHz sine at low gain with 2.5s decay routed straight to `masterGain`, bypassing `sfxAt`'s distance model because it is in your head, not the world, and duck `masterGain` to 0.15 for 0.6s. Ducking is what actually sells a bang.

While you are here, two rendering fixes with outsized value. `drawFx()` runs at 2144 and the fog fill at 2158-2159, so **every light emitter in the game is painted and then dimmed 86%** by `rgba(5,7,9,0.86)`. Firing in a dark corridor lights nothing. Split `drawFx()` into a pre-fog pass and a post-fog light-emitter pass with `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'`. And `tryFire` spawns the muzzle flash at `s.x + cos(ang)*(s.r+6)` — the body centre — while the round leaves `eyePoint(s)` at 1016. Your flagship mechanic is that your muzzle is not on your centreline, and you draw the muzzle flash on the centreline.

Also route `applySpall` (1034-1056) through the same feedback as a hit. It subtracts up to 11hp and pushes a single 3px spark for 0.1s, calling neither `sfxAt("hit")` nor `game.camKick` and pushing no blood, all three of which `applyHit` does. A player correctly using cover will conclude the game is damaging them at random.

**Why.** This changes no balance. It converts existing dead time from "the build is broken" into "I made a mistake", which is the difference between a playtest that produces tuning data and one that produces a bug list.

**Cost.** Small, all of it. Half a day including the fx split.

**How you know it worked.** Sam bangs himself and laughs instead of alt-tabbing to the console.

## The rest, by theme

### Theme A: The fight (after the top five)

1. **Rebuild the difficulty tiers on axes that decide fights.** `DIFFICULTIES` (2645-2649) varies exactly three values: `react`, `exec`, `surrender`. Enemy count, hp, damage, rpm, burst, spread, aimErr, sight, FOV, hearing, movement and every squad and player stat are byte-identical across all three. `react` provably decides nothing (100% win rate on ELITE and ROOKIE alike), so what actually separates the tiers is `exec`, a hard mission-failure countdown. **ELITE does not make the fighting harder; it makes deliberate play illegal**, which inverts the PRD's own "reflexes lose to preparation." Add per-tier `lead` (0.0/0.55/0.9), an `aimErr` multiplier at line 1492 (1.6/1.0/0.7), a sight multiplier (0.8/1.0/1.15), and the watching-the-door reaction. Keep `exec` near 30/24/20. Medium.
2. **Give shooters a lead solution.** `aimAngle(s, tx, ty)` (873) is three lines with no velocity term, and rounds are projectiles at 1300-1500 px/s. A player running at 148 px/s displaces 18px at 160px range and 55px at 480px against a 10px body radius. **Beyond roughly 88px, alternating strafe inputs make the entire enemy roster miss.** `updatePlayer` (907-913) already computes the movement delta and throws it away: store `p.vx/p.vy`, do the same for squad and enemy movers, lead by `flight = visD / weapon.speed` scaled by the per-tier `lead`. Apply it to the squad too or a strafing enemy becomes your problem in reverse the moment enemies start moving. Small, and it is the cleanest lever the tiers do not have.
3. **Amplify the corner game instead of adding to it.** `TUNE.eyeLateral` is 6px against a 32px tile, about ten screen pixels at zoom 1.6, shown by a 3.5px pip inside the operator's own silhouette. `makePlayer` (829) never calls `rollHandedness`, so it inherits `handed: "right"` and the HUD's LEFT SHOULDER branch is dead code, while enemies and squaddies do roll it. `swapShoulder` has exactly one caller, so no AI ever swaps and `TUNE.supportSideReact` can never apply to anyone (it has zero references in the file). And the re-shoulder cost is arithmetically near-zero: `supportSideSpread` 2.3 gives a 1.38-degree half-cone against a body subtending 1.79 degrees at 320px, so the shot is still guaranteed. Do this: when the player is within ~1.5 tiles of a wall corner, **draw a faint ghosted outline of the visibility polygon from the other shoulder.** One overlay teaches the whole mechanic without a word of text. Raise `eyeLateral` toward 9-10px. Move the swap cost from accuracy to time by wiring `supportSideReact` into the per-shot cooldown in `tryFire`, turning 0.094s into 0.127s, which puts it inside the alerted reaction window instead of comfortably outside it. Delete whichever of `supportSideSpread`/`supportSideReact` you do not use so the TUNE block stops lying. Medium. **Untested assumption:** that a human will ever notice or use the shoulder offset at all. This is your differentiator and it has never been in front of a person.
4. **Rate-limit the player's facing.** Line 917 is `p.face = angleTo(...)` with no smoothing. Every other actor in the file uses `angLerp` at rates between 2 and 12 (enemy at 1484, 1511, 1537, 1577, 1587; squad at 1889, 1908, 1917, 1926). Your operator rotates at infinite angular velocity while the men beside him turn like people. Use `angLerp(p.face, target, clamp(dt*22, 0, 1))` — about 110ms for a 180-degree reversal — and **leave `aimAngle` computing from the true cursor angle so no precision is lost; only the sprite lags.** This is the Door Kickers 2 reading of the fantasy rather than the Hotline Miami one, and your own research concludes the juice budget follows the fantasy. Pairs with turn bloom: if whipping the mouse costs accuracy, the body should visibly whip. Small. **Untested assumption:** that 110ms reads as mass and not as lag.

### Theme B: The squad (pillar 2 is currently failing, measurably)

1. **Break the huddle.** Instrumented over 24 bot runs across all six maps: the player fired 546 rounds, the squad fired 45 — **7.6% of team rounds.** `clearShot` allowed 369 of 9,117 engagement evaluations (4.0%), and the blocking body was a **teammate 7,898 times against the player only 850** — a 9:1 ratio. The cause is mutual occlusion inside the 80px follow huddle, not your back. `updateSquaddie` case `"follow"` (1907) is a single point-chase to 80px with no lateral slots. Replace it with a wedge: slot *i* holds a station at `player.face + 150deg + i*60deg` at 70-110px, recomputed as you turn, so no two operators share a bearing and nobody is on your bore line. Add an escape hatch: when a threat is acquired and `clearShot` returns false, strafe perpendicular to the shooter-target line for up to 40px before re-testing, with `clearShot` returning *which* body blocked so the strafe picks a direction. Two one-line fixes while you are in there: the acquisition at 1872 passes no facing argument to `canSee`, so squaddies acquire through 360 degrees at 520px while `visibleToPlayerSide` reports through a 160-degree cone — pass `s.face, deg(160)` so the squad shoots only what it reported; and the reload call at 1898 sits inside the `if (threat)` block, so a squaddie who breaks contact with three rounds stacks on the next door with three rounds. Medium.
2. **Let the player set a facing.** `orderMove` (1814-1823) bakes `face: angleTo(game.player.x, game.player.y, x, y)`, the bearing from wherever you happened to be standing. There is no input anywhere that sets a facing. **"Hold this corner covering the east hall" is not expressible**, and GOAL.md success criterion 3 is verbatim "My squad held that hallway exactly like I told them to." Make RMB a press-drag: release inside 6px keeps today's behaviour, drag further sets `order.face` and renders the arrow in world space beside the existing destination marker. Door Kickers 2 solved this in 2020 with two arrow types per waypoint, and your own research (ch.1 §2) records that tuning facing at each waypoint is the core skill of that game. ~30 lines. This is the highest-leverage control the squad layer is missing.
3. **Kill the ROE latch.** `game.alarm` is set in `alertEnemy` (1433) and the combat transition (1474), and reset only in `initGame` (2686) — grep confirms five references total and no mid-mission reset. `mayFire` (1892-1893) includes `|| game.alarm`, so **from the first alert, RETURN FIRE is behaviourally identical to WEAPONS FREE for the rest of the run.** Your V cycle offers three states and produces two outcomes. Drop `|| game.alarm` and fire under RETURN when the threat is in combat, has fired within 4s, or is visibly armed and closing. Give `e.alerted` a decay too (`e.alertT` from ~25s) — it is currently set once and never cleared (`e.alerted = false` appears exactly once, in the constructor at 1418), so "go quiet and reset" is not a strategy the game supports even though the noise system implies it is. Also fix `orderLabel` (2552), which returns "ENGAGING" whenever `s.engaged` is truthy regardless of ROE, so a HOLD FIRE squaddie standing there doing nothing reads as ENGAGING. Small, high value: this is your restraint control silently ceasing to exist ten seconds into every mission.
4. **Let the squad challenge suspects.** `squadShout` appears twice in the entire file: its definition at 1954 and one call at 1933, on entry arrival. Everywhere else a squaddie with a clean bead on a non-combat suspect stands mute and, once the alarm latch fires, shoots him. **Across 24 instrumented runs with the player never pressing C, total arrests = 0.** When a threat is acquired, `clearShot` passes, range is under ~260px and `threat.state !== "combat"`, call `squadShout` once per pair and open a 1.5s window during which RETURN FIRE will not engage that target; track `threat._challengedBy` so three men do not shout at once. ~20 lines, reuses `trySurrender` unchanged. SWAT 4's officers do this autonomously and it is the single loudest reason its 2005 AI is still praised; Ready or Not's teammates do not, and it is the most common complaint about them.
5. **Fix stack sides.** `orderStack` calls `stackSlots(d, game.player.x, game.player.y)` (1829), so the stack side comes from where *you* are standing. Stand inside a room, right-click the door you came through, and your squad stacks *inside the objective*. The bug is rare at spawn, when everyone is together, and common the moment you are the point man doing your job, which is the entire premise. Use the members' centroid, falling back to the player when the selection is empty. If the drag-to-face control ships, let a drag through the door override the side explicitly — that is Door Kickers 2's behaviour, where the approach vector comes from the drawn path. Also draw the three slot rings at order time rather than at execution time, so a wrong-side plan is visible before three men walk into a room. Small.
6. **Wire quiet movement.** `TUNE.squadWalk = 80` (line 151) has **zero references in the file.** All four `squadMove` call sites pass `TUNE.squadRun = 140`, and `moveAlongPath` emits footstep noise above 100. The squad is physically incapable of a quiet approach while you have Shift. Hold Shift while issuing an RMB order to mark it `quiet: true`. Small, and it pairs naturally with the drag-to-face control.
7. **Fix the utility economy.** `updatePlans` spends the breach charge via `p.members.find(s => s.alive)` (1994/2001) from a global pool, so **the BREACHER role confers nothing** — two SUPPORT gunners blow a door just as well (`s.role` is read in exactly one place, `squadSpriteFor` at 2247, for the sprite key). Line 2011 calls `throwBang(breacher, ...)` and decrements nothing, and `makeSquaddie` gives squaddies no bang inventory, so **there is no world state in which pressing T to disable the breach bang is correct** — a toggle with one useful position — and the DISTRACTION kit is a trap. Meanwhile `game.squadCharges` is hard-set to 2 regardless of kit while the player has a separate wall-charge count, and the HUD prints `CHG 2` for one and `CHARGE(2)` for the other. Put the charges on the BREACHER's person so losing him removes the option and the 12-point `squadLost` penalty means something; seed a shared bang pool from the kit; make bangs bite (8-15 damage inside 40px, 2s disoriented on hostages during which securing refuses) so T becomes a decision; label the pools `WALL CHG` / `TEAM CHG` / `TEAM BANGS`. Medium.

### Theme C: Information, stealth, and fairness (pillar 4)

1. **Make noise respect walls.** `processNoises` line 1669 is `if (dist(n.x, n.y, e.x, e.y) > n.radius) continue;` — pure Euclidean distance, no LOS test, no material attenuation, no door state. **A gunshot 560px away through six concrete walls alerts exactly as hard as one across an open room.** Closing a door behind you changes nothing; being two rooms deep changes nothing; you cannot pick a firing position for acoustic cover or open a far door to draw a patrol. The noise-ring visualisation teaches a rule the simulation does not implement. Walk the DDA and subtract per material: concrete 220, brick 160, sheetmetal 120, drywall 60, closed door 90, open/breached 0, broken window 0, glass 40. With 8 or fewer enemies this is trivially cheaper than the 280-ray visibility polygon you already run every frame. Retune the radii afterward (560 for a shot was tuned for a world with no walls) and give each PRIMARY its own value so the MP5's "poor through cover" penalty buys an actual stealth upside. Medium.
2. **Then add the propagation that is missing.** `processNoises` skips `n.side === "enemy"` entirely (1665), so **enemies cannot hear each other's gunfire**, and an enemy who has *seen* you tells nobody: the combat transition alerts only himself. On the combat transition, emit a callout noise at the sighting position and directly alert enemies within ~350px with LOS, passing the *target's* position rather than the caller's, rendered with the same expanding pulse so the player can watch information spread. Add a two-field blackboard (lastKnown, a set of searching room ids) so hunters claim different unsearched rooms instead of walking single file down the same corridor. Medium, and it is what gives the mirror and the quiet approach something to prevent.
3. **Draw what the simulation already knows.** `game.zoom` is 1.6, so the visible half-extent is `viewH/3.2`: **183px short of alerted sight at 1920x1080, 280px short at 1366x768.** Camera lead maxes at 110px toward the cursor and shrinks the opposite edge, so it never closes the gap. And nothing in `render()` draws a vision cone anywhere. Your own telemetry says only 4% of rounds that hit the player came from an off-screen shooter, so **fix the information before you touch the tuning**: draw the vision wedge of every enemy `visibleToPlayerSide()` returns (`e.alerted`, `e.face`, `aiFov/aiFovAlert` and `raycast` all exist), and add screen-edge threat chevrons for any enemy with LOS whose world position is outside the viewport rect. Only then consider clamping `aiViewDistAlert` to `min(520, viewH/(2*zoom) + 60)`. Medium.
4. **Make CHECK FIRE and `clearShot` round-aware.** `lineOfFireHazard` (2543) raycasts with `opaque` (2549) and stops at the first sight-blocking tile. Drywall is `opaque: true, resist: 5`; FMJ has `pen: 26`; and `parseLevel` auto-reclassifies every interior partition on every map as drywall (642-647). **The signature v0.5 interaction — hear a contact and shoot him through the partition — is exactly the case where CHECK FIRE cannot see the hostage on the far side.** FLIGHT 214's briefing warns about over-penetration killing hostages, about a hazard the HUD refuses to render; 37% of bystander hits (28 of 75) arrived through a wall. `clearShot` (1968) has the mirror hole: it tests only entity circles against the raw segment, never consults geometry, and never looks past the target, so it refuses a safe shot when a teammate stands behind concrete on the line and happily punches FMJ through drywall into a hostage it has never seen. Factor the barrier walk out of `resolveBarrier` (1060-1099) — it already does this arithmetic — and share it: accumulate `mat.resist` along the DDA, stop only past `AMMO[p.ammoType].pen`, sample three rays across the pellet or spread cone. Render the through-wall case as "CHECK FIRE — THROUGH WALL" with a dashed ring on the endangered body drawn above the fog. That teaches concealment-versus-cover in one image and is the first real reason to pick HP over FMJ. Small.
5. **Make the wall hatch a function of the loaded round.** Line 2069 hatches any wall with `mat.resist < 20`, which is genuinely good visual teaching, but penetration resolves as `b.pen > mat.resist`. FMJ (26) crosses drywall/wood/sheetmetal; AP (46) and SLUG (34) also defeat brick (30), which is never hatched; **9mm HP (3) and BIRDSHOT (2) defeat nothing, so every hatch on screen is a lie for those loadouts.** Hatch when `AMMO[player.ammoType].pen > mat.resist` and recompute on ammo change, so switching FMJ to HP at the briefing visibly redraws the map at the exact moment the player is deciding. Then set all three 5.56 `dmgMul` to 1.00 — FMJ (34) and HP (44.2) both kill a 65hp guard in exactly 2 rounds, so the damage axis is currently imperceptible — and let the whole decision live in penetration. Add a two-swatch legend to the briefing. Small.
6. **Make the mirror a decision.** `updatePlayer` 940-952: pressing Q at any closed door sets `game.mirror = {room: behind, t: 4}`. No uses, no cooldown, no time cost, no chance of being spotted, and the reveal is a whole flood-filled room regardless of whether a mirror under that door could physically see it. The PRD calls information "the scarcest resource"; it is the cheapest thing in the game, and it is not a decision because there is no reason not to press Q at every door. Put it in `UTILITY_KITS` with 2-3 uses so it trades against bangs and charges (Door Kickers 2 puts the mirror in an equipment slot for exactly this reason). Limit the reveal to a raycast cone from `doorCenter` within ±70 degrees of the door normal, capped at ~6 tiles. Charge 1.2s of held Q with no movement or fire. Add the missing else-branch hint (the key currently does nothing silently when the far side is exterior) and give mirror ghosts a distinct outline from squad-reported ghosts. Small.
7. **Fix the noise rings and give them sound.** `drawFx` clamps to `clamp(f.r * 0.42, 26, 210)`: shot 560, bang 700 and breach 760 all render at exactly 210. **The stealth game is entirely about relative loudness and the visualisation flattens the loud end into one size.** Use `26 + 60 * log2(1 + f.r/60)` for roughly 26/122/175/200/205. Your own emissions draw at alpha 0.55 with a 1.5px stroke while everyone else's draw at 0.7 with 2.5px dashed, so the sounds you are learning to control are the faint ones — swap that. And the whole system is mute: `emitFootstepNoise` (975) pushes an event for the AI and calls no `sfxAt`, `addNoise` (1397) has no audio hook at all. **In a game whose core loop is "what is behind this door", the ear is switched off.** Give `addNoise` an optional sfx name fired from the same call, with a per-voice lowpass keyed to opaque tiles crossed, reusing the attenuation walk from item 1. Small.

### Theme D: Objectives, scoring, and HUD honesty (pillar 5)

1. **Reprice the grade and show the ledger.** `computeGrade` (2808) is `100 + arrests*5 - kills*2 - civsKilledByUs*20 - squadLost*12 - surrenderedKilled*15`, S at 105. **Killing every suspect on a 7-man map and touching nothing else scores 86 — a passing B, on every one of the six missions.** S arrives at 3 arrests of 7 (43% restraint). Executing a surrendered suspect, your design's gravest ROE violation, costs 17 points and is fully earnable back: arrest 6 and execute 1 scores 113 = S. GOAL.md pillar 5 says the best run arrests more than it kills; the arithmetic disagrees. Make restraint a *gate*, the way SWAT 4 layers mission-failure rules on top of score: any `surrenderedKilled` or `civsKilledByUs` caps the run at C, S requires zero squad losses, and reprice so an all-kill clear cannot reach B (kills -6, civs -35, squadLost -15). A points-only system always lets the player buy back an atrocity. Then replace the eight raw counters with a signed timestamped ledger — `+5 ARREST 01:12`, `-15 SURRENDERED SUSPECT KILLED 01:40`, `-20 CIVILIAN KILLED (round penetrated drywall) 02:03` — plus the pass threshold and one line naming the largest single loss. `game.stats` is counters only, so push events from `killEntity`, `cuffEnemy` and `trySecureHostage`; the bullet already carries `penetrated` and `ricochet` flags from `resolveBarrier`, so the causal clause is free. Surface a running score top-right during play. Small. **Note:** do not justify this with "parking the squad is dominant." I tested it, 16 runs per configuration per map: COMPOUND 14/16 parked vs 9/16 following, but SAFEHOUSE 1/16 vs 6/16, THE PLANT 2/16 vs 12/16, MARKET 4/16 vs 5/16. Following wins on three of the four winnable maps. The scoring asymmetry is real; that tactical conclusion is not.
2. **Fix the compliance economy, because arrests are the only route above B.** I measured the shout directly: with more than one threat alive, **a cold shout at an idle guard converts 10.1% of the time and alerts him the other 89.9%** (4,000 trials at REGULAR). The alternative is 2 rounds in 0.094s, guaranteed. A failed shout calls `alertEnemy`, handing the target a reaction timer and latching `game.alarm`. The whole surrender/feint/cuff/ROE-scoring stack — five systems — hangs off a key that is a trap outside the post-bang window, and the scripted bot found that without being told. Four changes: (a) when the target is idle with no LOS to the shouter, skip `alertEnemy` and push him to `suspicious` with a 1.2s hesitation, so a shout costs nothing when you had the drop; (b) add a **silent compliance channel that uses the corner game you already built** — holding your muzzle on a non-combat suspect within ~120px for 0.8s rolls at outgunned odds, no key, no noise, so "get the drop on him" becomes the arrest verb; (c) fix the outgunned test, which counts any teammate with `canSee(t.x, t.y, e.x, e.y, 500)` with no facing argument and no ROE check, so a squaddie looking the other way on HOLD FIRE counts as a gun on him — pass `t.face, deg(120)` and count only shooters whose ROE would permit engaging; (d) replace the memoryless roll (currently 1.4s cooldown, unlimited uses, last suspect folds to a mean of 3.5 shouts) with a persistent `e.morale` that falls on buddy-killed-in-LOS, flashed, wounded, guns from more than one bearing, and last-man, and **rises ~0.08 per refused shout** so spamming C stops working. That is SWAT 4's model, and it gives the flashbang and the second angle readable value instead of burying them in a die roll. Finally, show the number: draw the computed probability over each enemy in the shout cone for 1.2s with the governing modifier ("10%", "55% FLASHED", "28% OUTGUNNED"). Medium.
3. **Make the execution timer honest.** `updateExecutionTimer` (2720) returns without decrementing whenever the taker is in combat, blind or staggered. `combat` requires only LOS to any one of your four, no shooting by either side, so **the game's only clock is fully paused, not slowed, by a state you can maintain indefinitely by parking a body in his view.** The PRD sells this as "buy time by attacking it"; the implementation grants unlimited time for free. Split it: blind and stagger stop the clock outright, combat slows it to 40% with a floor. Then render the held state as the loudest thing on screen, because it is the payoff for your central counterplay: strike the number through in green with "EXECUTION HELD — he's under fire" / "— he's flashed". Also label the grace reset at 2731, where the visible number jumps from 1 back to 6 with no explanation ("HE'S MOVING TO THEM — 6s"). Small.
4. **Mark the HVT and stop your own squad from failing the mission.** `OBJECTIVES.capture.failed()` ends the run the instant he dies. The only way to identify him is that `spriteFor` reuses `enemy_elite` tinted `#e8eef4` instead of `#b03a8c`, a colour code nothing explains, and he shoots at you alongside seven other hostiles who want shooting. `updateSquaddie`'s threat filter (1871) excludes only `surrender` and `cuffed`, and `clearShot` protects friendlies, hostages, civilians and surrendered suspects but has no capture-alive case — **nothing in the AI reads `game.mission.objectives` at all**, so your own team will fail the mission while you are in another building. Set `e.noEngage` at spawn from the objective list, have `clearShot` treat him as a blocker, have the squad shout instead of shoot, draw a persistent gold reticle whenever `visibleToPlayerSide` returns him (Hitman marks targets permanently for exactly this reason), and escalate CHECK FIRE to "HOLD — PRINCIPAL". Small.
5. **Resolve the extract objective's three-way disagreement.** HIGH VALUE's brief says "Take the principal alive and walk him to the vehicle." `OBJECTIVES.extract.done()` (2766) checks only `inExtraction(game.player)`. And a cuffed HVT cannot walk anywhere: `updateEnemy` returns immediately for `state === "cuffed"` (1441), verified headlessly. Meanwhile `label()` returns "EXFIL AT POINT" from the player's position alone while `done()` also requires every other objective, so you stand on the green tile reading AT POINT while nothing happens. The interesting fix is the brief's version: give cuffed suspects an `escortedBy` field, press E to attach, move at 0.7x while attached, require `inExtraction(hvt)`. ~40 lines, and it turns a walk-back into the escort the pitch describes. The cheap fix is to correct the label and rewrite the brief. Ship either; do not ship the current disagreement. Medium.
6. **Tint corpses by side.** `spriteFor(e, dead)` returns `{key: "corpse", tint: COLORS.corpse}` before any side check, so a dead hostage, a dead operator, an executed surrendered suspect and a dead patrolman render identically — **four categories `computeGrade` scores separately and the world refuses to distinguish.** One line: tint by the entity's living colour dimmed ~45%. Then add a 0.35s settle (count `ent.deadT`, interpolate scale 1 → 0.85 with a rotation seeded at death) and a pooling decal, because bodies currently snap from standing to dead on the tile lattice. SWAT 4's rooms tell their story after the fight; yours are illegible until a debrief screen you cannot navigate away from. Small.

### Theme E: Teaching

**Build the cheap half of the teaching layer, not a tutorial campaign.** The entire teaching surface is a 28-binding key table on the menu plus 13 `game.hint` assignments, **most of which fire after a failed action**: "No wall charges left", "That wall is structural", "Clear the room before securing". The only proactive channel is `contextPrompt` (2561), covering four situations, one of which currently crashes the game. The two verbs that most need prompting are absent: **C**, the entire arrest economy and the only route above B, and **X**, the GO key that is the signature moment the whole pitch is built on. Q, N, G, H, B, T, V and Z are surfaced nowhere in-mission.

Extend `contextPrompt` into a priority list evaluated against world state showing at most one line, plus a second `game.offer` slot that fires once per mechanic per profile and persists in the same localStorage bucket as best grades. Concrete predicates, all computable from existing state: any stack in `ready` → "[X] GO — 2 stacks set"; an uncuffed surrendered suspect in LOS → "[E] cuff him before he re-arms"; a visible enemy with `blind > 0` → "[C] shout now — a flashed suspect folds five times more often"; two or more friendly guns on a non-combat enemy → "[C] shout — he's outgunned"; facing a wall with charges and a squad selected → "[H] plant charge"; a closed door with squaddies selected → "[RMB] stack them here". Suppress each after the player performs that verb three times. 40-60 lines, no new maps. Medium.

### Theme F: Hygiene

1. **Wire or delete the four dead TUNE constants.** Zero references outside their definitions: `squadWalk` (151), `civilianRun` (153), `bangSurrenderBonus` (186), `supportSideReact` (199). `civilianRun` is the tell that **hostages and civilians never move at all** — `updateCivilian` (1655) ticks blind and stagger and writes `c.cower`, which nothing reads — so they are static props, which removes the whole class of decisions a hostage-rescue game runs on: the civilian who bolts across your line, the hostage who stands up when the bang goes. On `game.alarm`, path civilians at `civilianRun` to the nearest tile 3+ tiles from any live enemy and out of the line of recent fire; read `cower` as a body-radius shrink plus a movement lock. Delete `bangSurrenderBonus` (superseded by `surrenderFlashed` 0.55). **Every one of these already has a name in TUNE, which means the design intent survived and only the wiring is missing — the cheapest design work available in the build.** Small.
2. **Key persistence by a stable map id.** `saveBest` writes under `mapIdx + ":" + diffKey` and `refreshMenu` reads back by button position. The moment anything inserts or reorders a mission — which several recommendations above would motivate — every stored record from that index onward points at a different mission. Add `id: "compound"` etc. to each MAPS entry, key by `id + ":" + diffKey`, bump the namespace to `tdt_save_v2` and drop the old bucket rather than migrating; the existing data is one letter per map. Small, low impact today, and it stops being low the moment anything worth keeping lands in that store.

## Do not do these

**Do not nerf player damage or raise enemy HP to fix the duel.** The player already wins every fight; lengthening it makes you win the same fight more slowly. The problem is that acquisition is free, not that your rifle is strong. Put the cost on the time between seeing and shooting.

**Do not gate enemy acquisition on the screen rect.** It is the obvious fix for the sight-versus-viewport gap and it is wrong: it makes a player who shrinks their browser window literally harder to see, and it means the same tactical situation resolves differently on a laptop and a monitor. Fix the information (cones, chevrons, directional damage), then clamp the range against a resolution-independent formula if it still bites.

**Do not build the eight-beat tutorial campaign with dedicated teaching maps.** It is a large content project resting on a core loop the measurements say is not yet proven, and every hour of it would need rebuilding after the acquisition model changes. Build the contextual offer layer instead: 60 lines, zero new maps, and it survives a rebalance.

**Do not build the next four missions on the ▶ NEXT line of BURNDOWN.md** (ship boarding, extreme rendition, assassination, compound infiltration). You have six missions, five of which are unreachable after one completion, two of which the bot has never won, and one of which deploys a one-man squad. **Content is not the constraint. Nothing in the build is short of maps.** The mission framework is proven; wait until the fight underneath it is worth playing six times.

**Do not add the non-lethal arsenal** (taser / beanbag / pepperball, PRD §6 rank 3). The arrest game's problem is not a missing weapon, it is a 10.1% dice roll on a key whose failure mode is starting a firefight, a squad that never challenges anybody, and a grade that pays a 7-point swing for a 90% risk. Adding a taser to that economy converts the dominant strategy from "shoot him" into "shoot him with a taser." Fix the compliance model first; then a taser is a real choice.

**Do not add a lean/peek key to strengthen the corner game.** You already compute the asymmetry every frame — `eyePoint()` originates the visibility polygon and the firing solution at the shoulder while enemies test the body centre. The mechanic is not missing, it is invisible: 6px, about ten screen pixels, marked by a 3.5px pip inside the operator's own sprite. Draw the other-shoulder visibility polygon as a ghost and raise `eyeLateral`. A new input adds a thing to learn; the overlay makes the thing you already built perceptible.

**Do not use the assault bot as a balance oracle.** It found a real dominant strategy (shooting beats shouting), and it has also produced at least one confident wrong answer: the claim that gating its trigger on `lineOfFireHazard` takes FLIGHT 214 from 0 to 26/30 wins does not reproduce — I ran it at walk speed with a `!lineOfFireHazard(P)` gate, 20 runs, and got **0/20 wins with more hostage deaths, 20 versus 16 stock**, exactly as the hazard scan's penetration blindness predicts. Keep the bot for regression and for measuring the simulation. Do not let it decide what is fun.

**Do not start the campaign, progression, or persistence layer.** It is already a stated non-goal until the core loop is proven, the loop is not proven, and your save format is keyed by array index anyway.

**Do not decide the title yet.** BURNDOWN lists theming as unblocked because "mechanics are proven." They are not proven; they are implemented. Those are different words.

## Proposed sequence for the next three sessions

**Session 1 — Make it survivable, then put a human in front of it.**
`closeDoor`'s missing `c`, the `frame()` try/catch and SIMULATION ERROR state, the bot case that closes doors and the removal of bot.js's swallow. Debrief → Mission Select, plus Escape handled in the debrief state. HIGH VALUE's two `s` glyphs and the `validateMap` rule that prevents the class. Window tiles in the flood fill, the local threat gate for securing, prone hostages that walk out, `rescue.failed()` counting only unsecured deaths. Blind/stagger/damage HUD, the pre-fog/post-fog `drawFx` split, muzzle flash at `eyePoint`, spall feedback, corpse tint by side, player facing rate-limit.

Then stop coding. **Play all six missions on REGULAR yourself and write down every sentence you say out loud.** That playtest is the deliverable of session 1, not the code. Everything in session 2 is currently justified by a harness that aims perfectly and never takes cover, and you should know before you build it whether the game already feels different to a person than it does to the bot.

**Session 2 — Make the fight losable.**
Turn bloom in `currentSpread`. The awareness accumulator replacing the boolean. The watching-the-door reaction state. Enemies breaking contact on damage, holding the angle short of doorways, and pairing up on a target. Bullet lead with a per-tier skill factor, applied to the squad too. Then rebuild `DIFFICULTIES` on `lead` / `aimErr` / sight / door-watching and pull `exec` back toward 30/24/20. Close the session by re-running the duel harness across all 15 cells, and by playing THE COMPOUND again and checking whether the doorway camp still works.

**Session 3 — Make the squad a second player, and make restraint pay.**
The follow wedge, the `clearShot` strafe escape hatch, the facing argument on squad acquisition, the reload moved out of the threat block. RMB press-drag for order facing, Shift for quiet orders, stack sides from the members' centroid. Drop `|| game.alarm` from `mayFire` and fix `orderLabel`. Autonomous squad challenges with the 1.5s no-fire window. Then the grade gates and the timestamped debrief ledger, and the compliance rework if the session has room — the cold-shout fix and the muzzle-hold arrest channel are the two pieces that matter most.

**Deferred to session 4 and beyond, in this order:** suppression plus a base-of-fire order (the single addition that would make the player and the squad do genuinely different jobs at the same time, and the thing that would make the SUPPORT belt-fed mean something), noise attenuation through walls with enemy callouts, the contextual offer layer, penetration-aware CHECK FIRE and `clearShot`, vision cones and edge chevrons, the mirror as a limited kit item.

**Untested assumptions carried through all three sessions, stated plainly:** that the doorway camp is what a human actually does (the bot does not corner at all); that the strafe-peek exploit is one a human will find (it is the most likely one, but that is inference from other games, not from this one); that the 4% off-screen-shooter figure survives a human who moves and looks differently than a bot; that the corner game is perceptible or wanted by anyone; that turn bloom and facing lag read as weight; that any of the six missions is winnable by a human on ELITE, since two are not winnable by the bot on any setting; and that the arrest loop is fun once it stops being a trap. Session 1 exists specifically to start converting that list into measurements.
